# Research framework and scoring

## Evidence grades

- `High`: primary filing/rule/data, independently cross-checked, current and definitionally aligned.
- `Medium`: credible secondary data or a derived value with disclosed assumptions.
- `Low`: single-source estimate, survey, anonymous report, social narrative or incomplete history.

Do not turn low-grade evidence into a high-confidence action by repeating it across agents.

## Decision scorecard

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 and explain the evidence. Do not mechanically average away a fatal flaw.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business/moat | structurally impaired | viable, mixed durability | durable and strengthening |
| Financial quality | weak cash/accounting/leverage | acceptable, cyclical or uneven | strong cash conversion and returns |
| Management/governance | trust or allocation failure | adequate with concerns | credible, aligned, disciplined |
| Industry/cycle | adverse structure | balanced/mid-cycle | favorable structure/runway |
| Valuation | upside requires heroic assumptions | near fair value | strong margin of safety |
| Market/timing | breakdown/poor liquidity | neutral/base building | confirmed trend with favorable asymmetry |
| Evidence confidence | sparse/conflicting | adequate | primary, current, cross-checked |

## Fatal and serious red lines

Treat these as potential overrides:

- verified fraud, disclosure unreliability or management integrity failure;
- solvency/refinancing risk that can permanently impair equity;
- structural loss of license, core customer, technology relevance or moat;
- thesis depending on an accounting metric that does not convert to cash;
- valuation requiring implausible growth with no margin of safety;
- trading halt, delisting path, inaccessible shares or liquidity too low for the plan;
- evidence too weak to identify the security or calculate a legal order.

Distinguish `fatal`, `serious but monitorable`, and `ordinary volatility`.

## Valuation method selection

- Stable compounder: owner earnings/FCF DCF plus justified earnings multiple.
- Cyclical: mid-cycle margin and normalized earnings; do not value peak earnings at a peak multiple.
- Bank/insurer: book value, ROE, capital adequacy and normalized credit/underwriting cycle.
- Commodity/resource: cycle-normalized price/cost curve, reserves and balance-sheet survival.
- Early growth: unit economics, dilution and scenario value; avoid false precision when FCF is distant.
- Holding company: sum of parts with tax, debt and holding-company discount.

Use conservative/base/optimistic assumptions. Explain growth, margin, multiple/discount rate, dilution and horizon. Compare current price with each scenario and show expected downside as seriously as upside.

## Technical timing controls

- Use exact dated OHLCV data and a consistent adjusted series.
- Prefer a small complementary set: trend (20/50/200-day), momentum, volume/relative strength and volatility. Avoid indicator voting.
- Support/resistance must come from visible swing areas, volume/price acceptance or moving-average structure, not round-number intuition.
- A breakout requires price and volume/relative-strength confirmation; a pullback entry requires support to hold.
- ATR-based risk is a volatility guide, not a thesis stop.

## Thesis health

Maintain 3-7 testable assumptions and company-specific red lines. Rate each assumption `intact`, `weakened`, `damaged`, or `broken`. Price decline alone does not break a thesis; a broken thesis is not repaired by a lower price.
